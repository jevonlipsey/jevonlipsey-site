## Overview

Social robotics research often defaults to structured institutional spaces: eldercare facilities, hospitals, classrooms, and corporate offices. In this work, we ask what it means to design social robotic systems that genuinely support unhoused individuals without becoming mechanisms of municipal policing, surveillance, or urban displacement.

## Core Questions
- How can robotic agents offer tangible mutual aid (e.g. power distribution, secure temporary storage, information brokering) while explicitly preventing law enforcement co-optation?
- What physical morphologies and interaction modalities signal non-coercive agency?
- How do we conduct participatory design with unhoused community members with true ethical reciprocity and dignity preservation?
